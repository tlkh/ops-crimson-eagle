# Chinook exterior geometry reference notes

Updated 10 October 2026. The procedural exterior in `src/render/aircraft.ts` represents the RSAF CH-47F and the JGSDF CH-47JA. It is a game-resolution reconstruction from published dimensions and photographs, not a manufacturer CAD model or a specific registered airframe.

## Sources inspected

- [Boeing H-47 Chinook](https://www.boeing.com/defense/military-rotorcraft/h-47-chinook): overall dimensions; 18.3 m rotor diameter, roughly 15.6 m fuselage, 30.1 m rotor-operating length, 5.7 m height. The current page describes Block II, so only family-level exterior dimensions were used, not a claim that these game aircraft are Block II.
- [Kawasaki CH-47J/JA](https://www.khi.co.jp/mobility/aero/helicopters/ch_47j.html): manufacturer describes the enlarged JA fuel tanks and gives 30.2 m operating length, 18.3 m width and 5.7 m height. Its [JGSDF photograph](https://www.khi.co.jp/mobility/aero/helicopters/img/ch_47j_im03.jpg) was inspected directly, not shipped as a game asset. It establishes the nose radome, flat cockpit glazing, tall aft pylon, engine location, fuel tanks, circular windows and green/brown camouflage. The national disc sits low on the aft tank side beneath the rear cabin windows.
- [Singapore MINDEF, full operational capability announcement](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/11apr24_nr/): confirms RSAF CH-47F and supplies operational photographs. The image CDN did not yield usable photos in this environment.
- [PIONEER / Singapore MINDEF, RSAF capability article](https://defencepioneer.sg/pioneer-articles/rsaf-to-continue-to-invest-in-capabilities---tech--chief-of-air-force): its [large operational photograph](https://defencepioneer.sg/images/mindefpioneerlibraries/default-source/articles/people/2022/14-feb-%283%29-2022/14feb22_news3_photo4.jpg?sfvrsn=76464a62_2) was inspected directly. The side view shows enlarged tanks on the RSAF F, circular cabin windows, forward twin wheels, aft single wheels, nose radar, chin sensor, engine intakes facing forward at the aft pylon and rear-facing metal exhausts.
- [U.S. Army CH-47D Operator's Manual, §2-13](https://aviation-assets.info/wp-content/uploads/TM_1-1520-240-10_Operators_Manual_for_ARMY_-CH-47D_HELICOPTER.pdf): family-level lighting reference. It places green/red position lamps on the right/left fuselage, a white position lamp on the aft pylon's vertical panel, red anti-collision strobes above the aft pylon and below the fuselage, and two controllable searchlights beneath the fuselage.
- [U.S. Army CH-47D Maintenance Test Flight Manual](https://chinook-helicopter.com/Publications/CH-47D_Technical_Publications/Maintenance_Test_Flight_Manual/TM_1-1520-240-MTF.pdf): its exterior inspection checklist identifies a position light on the forward landing-gear support structure. This corroborates the forward support location as a family reference; it does not establish an exact mount coordinate for either export variant.

## Corrections made

- Replaced the long ellipsoid with a rounded, nearly constant cargo-cabin section, sloping chin and upswept rear closure.
- Shortened the fuselage and separated the tandem mast centres by 11.85 m; the 18.29 m rotor discs produce the documented approximately 30.14 m operating envelope. Both rotors have three blades and counterrotate.
- Added the low forward and distinctly taller aft transmission pylons, plus the longitudinal driveshaft fairing.
- Moved both engines from the cabin roof to the sides of the aft pylon; added forward intakes and rear exhausts.
- Replaced rectangular cabin windows with circular portholes. Used flat, separately framed windscreen, pilot-side and lower nose panes.
- Rebuilt the fuel tanks as rounded, tapered sponsons. Both export variants have enlarged tanks, matching the inspected photographs. Their approximate 4.8 m width should not be confused with Boeing's baseline 3.8 m fuselage-width figure.
- Reference photographs show four landing legs with twin forward wheels and single aft wheels (six tyres total); the earlier description of four single wheels was incorrect. The cargo hook and closed cargo ramp that tapers upward and inward toward the tail remain part of the representative game configuration.
- Used olive RSAF paint and a broad disruptive green/brown JGSDF pattern with red/white roundels. Omitted invented registration numbers, squadron insignia and bright rescue-orange stripes. Exact camouflage boundaries and small markings are interpretive.
- Merged stationary geometry by material to keep rendering cost suitable for a mobile browser. The animated rotor/disc API is unchanged.
- Capped the loft ends, especially the rear transmission pylon, after a rear-view browser screenshot exposed a see-through opening. The aft closure now reads as solid behind the rotor mast.

## Accuracy limits

Only overall published dimensions are dimensionally constrained. Cross sections, local panel positions, pylon widths and equipment sizes are photograph-based estimates. The cargo ramp is fixed in a mostly raised pose beneath a shallow, bounded upper opening; only a simplified recess is modelled, not a full cabin or articulated loading system. No rotor articulation, rivet-level detailing or exact aircraft-specific equipment fit is claimed. Rotor blur is a rendering effect, not an aerodynamic model. Official source photographs are references only and are not redistributed in the shipped game.

## Rear-view and livery refinement, 9 October 2026

A further pass directly inspected the large PIONEER RSAF photograph linked above and Kawasaki's JGSDF photograph. The [MINDEF CH-47F fact sheet](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/news-and-events/latest-releases/11apr24_fs/) independently confirms the 30.14 m operating length, 18.29 m rotor diameter and 5.68 m height. Its migrated image endpoint was unavailable, so the PIONEER photograph was the Singapore visual reference. A DVIDS JGSDF deck-landing reference was located but could not be opened, and is not counted as inspected visual evidence.

Changes concentrate on the normal rear chase-camera view:

- The aft pylon now has a wider, less pinched rear face, a fully closed cap, restrained access-cover/grille detail and a continuous painted surface. End-cap normals are separated from the side shell.
- The rear cargo closure uses painted, capped surfaces and a narrow seam rather than a contrasting tan slab or visible hole. The lower fuselage rises and narrows into the closed ramp.
- Rear exhausts have open-ended metal sleeves, rolled rims, sleeve bands and recessed dark faces. Their sleeves no longer end in solid bright circular caps. Small aft protection housings are included on the RSAF variant, informed by the operational photo.
- Wheel locations and tyre contact height remain unchanged at approximately -2.52 m, preserving the ship-deck clearance correction.

Two separate authored texture/material sets are generated when each campaign's aircraft is created:

1. **RSAF CH-47F:** muted olive-grey paint, subtle panel joints/fasteners, low-contrast paint variation and a roughness map; low-visibility English service lettering on the long-range tanks.
2. **JGSDF CH-47JA:** tan-dominant paint with broad sage-green and near-black-green disruptive fields, matching the relative colour balance and large patch scale in the supplied side, overhead and aft-quarter references; the same restrained surface treatment and roughness map; white-edged red national discs and Japanese service lettering. The broad, irregular camouflage fields replace the previous sinusoidal vertex-colour stripes.

Each set uses one 1024-square colour atlas, one 128-square roughness atlas, and 512-square normal and ambient-occlusion maps. Geometry supplies explicit UVs, including closed end faces, and material batching preserves those UVs. Markings remain separately mapped meshes. The atlases are authored shapes and paint detail; no official photograph is embedded or redistributed. No aircraft serial, unit marking or exact lion insignia is fabricated. The viewed aircraft photos show side/oblique views, so rear panel dimensions, small latch locations and exact camouflage boundaries remain representative interpretations, not a measured rear orthographic reconstruction.

The subsequent user-provided photographs showed the first JGSDF atlas was too green and its pattern too fine. The fuselage atlas uses a tan base and fewer larger organic green fields, with sparse near-black-green accents along tan/green boundaries. Edge wrapping keeps the side camouflage continuous. The later user request makes the tapered aft fuselage, closed rear cap and aft pylon solid green, without a tan rear patch. JGSDF non-skinned trim remains green. The supplied photographs were used only as visual references, not copied into game assets.

## Earlier focused rear-reference pass

The additional user-supplied rear photograph informed the tall aft pylon and the recessed exhaust nozzles with cross braces. The earlier side-profile drawing and user direction established a **closed ramp and tapered tail**. The later slot-opening request below supersedes that solid end-cap configuration. The fuselage narrows from 1.42 m to 0.70 m half-width and its lower line rises from about -1.3 m to -0.31 m near the rear cap; the aft pylon's rear roof slopes down to meet it. The end cap is solid, so terrain cannot show through. The tapered loft itself forms the closure; offset ramp panels and loose edge pieces were removed after a side-view inspection. Following the latest rear-view feedback, the aft pylon and tapered rear closure use a solid green material; the S-shaped tan/dark motif and subsequent tan patch are gone. Exhaust mouths have small cross braces and recessed centres. Earlier gear work added upper fairings, load struts and diagonal braces, while preserving the -2.52 m tyre contact height for ship-deck clearance. The 10 October close-up exposed upper gear mounts detached from the aft hull; the correction is recorded in the implemented exterior pass below. These are still game-resolution photo estimates, not measured rear orthographic data.

The earlier flight-RPM pass hid distinct blades behind translucent swept rotor discs. Current gameplay keeps the physical blades visible alongside the blur, with both using simulation time. A later close-up revealed that the four thin rods apparently protruding through the JGSDF roof were actually the separate bucket's support straps. The bucket had been labelled as staged ashore but positioned at the helicopter while unattached. Its initial and ongoing simulation position now stays at the shore handling site; rendering also derives that position from the shore marker so older saved sorties do not show the rods.

The rotor blur uses a feathered radial transparency texture with short curved sweep marks. Its stronger flight opacity makes both discs legible against sea and deck from the chase camera. Slow deck rotation keeps the distinct blades visible over a lighter disc.

## Roof and landing-gear cleanup

The two freestanding 0.48 m centreline roof rods and a round roof lump were removed from both game variants. They were unsupported details that looked like oversized aerials from the chase view. The low synchronising-driveshaft fairing remains. An earlier landing-gear pass replaced spherical joints with compact mounts, oleo struts, braces and hub forks, while keeping the tyre contact plane near -2.52 m for ship-deck alignment.

The earlier note that the aircraft has one wheel at each of four legs was wrong. The directly inspected [RSAF PIONEER photograph](https://defencepioneer.sg/images/mindefpioneerlibraries/default-source/articles/people/2022/14-feb-%283%29-2022/14feb22_news3_photo4.jpg?sfvrsn=76464a62_2) and [Kawasaki JGSDF photograph](https://www.khi.co.jp/mobility/aero/helicopters/img/ch_47j_im03.jpg) show twin forward wheels and single aft wheels: four legs and six tyres. Boeing's [CH-47F landing-test article](https://www.boeing.com/features/2026/04/ch-47f-chinook-completes-first-supervised-autonomy-landing) describes a touchdown on all four wheels; that sentence describes the test landing and is not a parts breakdown. Use variant photographs for visible wheel configuration. Army CH-47D manuals provide family-level component and lighting evidence only.

The 10 October 2026 audit screenshot shows the aft gear mounting blocks stopping short of the tapered hull, so the legs read as detached. Correct the mount-to-hull connection while preserving the six-tyre arrangement and contact plane. The screenshot also exposes position lamps too far aft on the tanks, cockpit panes intersecting the opaque nose shell, and engine/intake and mirrored-door shapes that need closer comparison to the variant photographs. The Army lighting manuals support a forward landing-gear-support location for the position lamps, a white position light on the aft pylon's vertical panel, red anti-collision lights above the aft pylon and beneath the fuselage, and two searchlights beneath the fuselage. Treat these as CH-47-family references, check export-specific fittings against RSAF and JGSDF photographs, and do not assign exact measured coordinates from oblique images.

The representative variants remain an olive RSAF CH-47F and a green/brown JGSDF CH-47JA. The raised lower ramp and tapered rear are retained below the upper slot; the JGSDF aft surfaces keep the user's specified solid green finish.

## Implemented exterior corrections, 10 October 2026

- **Nose and glazing:** a faceted cockpit shell replaces the old overlapping opaque loft and window panels. The cheeks narrow toward a raised chin; a tapered radar housing overlaps the nose instead of appearing as a separate black ball. Windshield posts are narrow and pane normals are consistent across their triangles. The RSAF chin sensor remains seated against the revised belly. This is a photograph-based silhouette refinement, not a measured canopy reconstruction.
- **Landing gear:** compact tapered support fairings bury their inner edges in the hull/tanks and connect to braced struts. Forward legs carry paired tyres and aft legs single tyres. Axle centres, longitudinal placement and the -2.52 m tyre contact plane remain unchanged, preserving deck alignment. This resolves the detached aft mounts shown in the user's close-up.
- **Lights:** model-owned fixtures replace duplicated render-time bulbs. Red port and green starboard position lamps move from the aft tanks to the forward tank/gear-support area; the white aft light sits on the aft pylon panel. Red beacons sit above the aft pylon and beneath the fuselage, with two forward/downward belly lamp housings. Dark caps screen the back of directional lenses. Night illumination consumes these mount transforms and beacon pulses use simulation time. Exact export-variant mount coordinates remain photo estimates informed by the family manuals.
- **JGSDF markings:** both roundels move from the upper cabin to the aft tank sides below the last cabin windows. Their geometry follows the tank surface rather than floating on a flat plane. Service lettering remains on the tanks, without invented serials or unit insignia.
- **Remaining exterior:** stepped engine nacelles with recessed intakes, refined pylon profiles, swept blade tips, and batched bearing collars/pitch links improve the silhouette and stopped-rotor views. The starboard crew entrance differs from the port emergency hatch. Sparse paint joints replace the regular square panel grid, and localized end-cap UVs avoid tiny repeated camouflage patches.

`scripts/aircraft/index.html` is a development-only inspection fixture using the actual `createAircraft` model. It provides both variants, orthographic/quarter/gear views, day/dusk/night lighting, stopped/slow/flight rotors and pause. It is not part of the production entry point. Mesh-level regression checks cover six-tyre arrangement/contact height, solid backing behind every light housing and cockpit glazing free of opaque skin. Lighting checks cover mount orientation, colours, reproducible pulses and resource ownership.

Verification on 10 October: the nine aircraft/lighting tests passed in the shared checkout. An isolated snapshot of the committed project plus the aircraft changes passed all 179 tests and the production build; the shared checkout's full build was blocked by concurrent scenery edits in `landUse.ts` and its tests. The isolated production build was checked at `/ops-crimson-eagle/` at 1440×1000 and 390×844, including both campaigns, mission launch, campaign switching, pause and resume. These are authored game dimensions chosen to reproduce the visible proportions, not measurements recovered from the photograph. The recess closes at Z=7.60 m so neither sky nor terrain can show through.
